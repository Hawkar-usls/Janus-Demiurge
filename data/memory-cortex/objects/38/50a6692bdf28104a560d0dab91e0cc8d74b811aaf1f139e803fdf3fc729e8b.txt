TRUST-Balance: a risk-constrained, utility-guided learning framework for adaptive and scalable imbalanced classification

This repository holds the reference Python implementation of TRUST-Balance (Trustworthy Risk-adaptive Utility and STructure-preserving Balancing), a model-agnostic framework for imbalanced classification. TRUST-Balance treats class-imbalance handling as risk-constrained intervention selection rather than fixed resampling. It splits the minority class into regions with K-means. For each region it chooses between screened synthetic generation, localised reweighting and abstention, scoring each candidate by its estimated reduction in class-balanced validation risk. That score is penalised for uncertainty, structural distortion and cost, and is subject to a tolerance on majority-class loss. The procedure stops when no intervention gives a positive utility. The estimator is scikit-learn compatible and works with any probabilistic classifier. It has three optional modes: a post-selection refit (TRUST+R), a Hoeffding-type acceptance certificate (TRUST-C), and subsampled screening that scales to millions of records. The archive contains the estimator and re-implementations of three recent adaptive baselines (ART, UGO and GDEO). It also includes a script that downloads the 27 public benchmark datasets from KEEL, PMLB/UCI and scikit-learn, plus the full experimental pipeline. The pipeline benchmarks logistic regression, random forests and XGBoost against 13 competing approaches, including the unmodified model. It also runs ablation, safeguard stress tests, sensitivity, robustness to imbalance ratio, label noise and class overlap, multiclass extension, certification and scalability studies up to one million records. The archive also ships the raw per-run results as CSV, the summary tables, the figures, and analysis scripts that reproduce every table and figure of the accompanying article. Those statistics include Friedman/Iman–Davenport tests, Nemenyi critical differences and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests. Code is released under the MIT licence and results under CC BY 4.0.
