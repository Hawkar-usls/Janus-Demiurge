The Ferromagnet as a New Mathematical Theorem: The Partition Function Is Its Own Fold for Every System of Spins with a Flip, the Circle Is the Line of That Fold, and the Zeros of Two and of Three Spins Lie on It Exactly Inside the Bound

This paper proves, in Lean 4 with Mathlib, what is forced about a system of spins that has a flip. A system is any finite set of states with a map of the states to themselves that is its own inverse, a weight the map keeps, and a count of up spins that the map sends to its complement. No lattice, no dimension, no range and no sign of the coupling is assumed. For every such system the partition function, as a polynomial in the activity $z$, is its own fold: its value at $1/z$ times $z^n$ is its value at $z$. Its zeros therefore come in folded pairs. The circle of unit modulus is the line of that fold: a point of the plane other than zero is kept by the map $z \mapsto 1/\bar z$ exactly when it is on the circle. For two spins the zeros are on the circle exactly when the coupling is inside its bound, and outside the bound every zero is real and off the circle. For three spins on a triangle the polynomial factors, and its zeros are on the circle inside the corresponding bound. In every such system any reading the flip reverses has total zero against the weight, so a finite system in no field has no net magnetisation. The file has 17 theorems, declares no axiom and leaves no proof open. That the zeros lie on the circle for every number of spins with ferromagnetic coupling is the theorem of Lee and Yang [2]; it is cited and is not proved here.
