Norm Formation And Relational Dynamics In Heterogeneous Communities: A Field-Theoretic Discussion

This note develops a mathematical proposal for studying norm formation among heterogeneous subjects, including humans and AI systems. It begins from a five-level taxonomy of behavioral description and distinguishes dynamical interaction, evidence, social uptake, and order-relative normative standing. Subjects retain different state spaces and update mechanisms. Relations can introduce mediated interactions, change existing couplings, create institutional powers, alter observation channels, and enable new joint practices. A causal stochastic formulation accommodates dissipation, learning, memory, partial observation, and discrete institutional events. Variational actions form a useful subclass. Norm content is represented separately from recognition, enforcement, and behavioral efficacy. Explicit constructions cover prohibitions, obligations, permissions, constitutive rules, powers, revision, and conflicts. Five short derivations establish the limits of gradient descriptions, the effect of finite penalties, the memory retained after eliminating a mediator, an identification problem, and an order of intervention capacities. A worked approval-and-release example connects the mathematics to human–AI community design. A brief extension shows how an implemented normative act can alter a feedback loop and cross a collective stability threshold; a companion paper develops criticality and phase-transition phenomena independently. The constructions are proposed modeling choices; their empirical adequacy requires independent investigation.
