An exact recurrence for extremal binary deletion-ball intersections

The maximum intersection of two deletion balls determines the worst-case number of distinct reads needed for unique reconstruction in a deletion-correcting code. Let $N(n,\ell,s)$ be this maximum for pairs of binary words of length $n$ with longest common subsequence of length at most $n-\ell$. Fix the separation $\ell$ and radius cap $T$ with $1\leq\ell\leq T$. For every sufficiently large $n$, one pair attains the maxima simultaneously at radii $0\leq s\leq T$. These maxima are the first $T+1$ coefficients of $\lambda(z)^n C_{\ell,T}(z)$, where $\lambda(z)=(1+\sqrt{1+4z})/2$. In particular, $N(n,\ell,t)=N(n-1,\ell,t)+N(n-2,\ell,t-1)$ eventually for every fixed $\ell\leq t\leq T$. In a greedy subsequence scan, extending any sufficiently long alternating interval multiplies the intersection profile by the same factor $\lambda(z)$. A finite comparison of profiles at a common length computes the integer polynomial $C_{\ell,T}$ and the cutoff. At separation three and fixed radius at least five, the resulting upper bound matches the first two terms of the known lower bound.
