Why the System of Eternity Is Built This Way: A design-rationale account, from a challenge to "perfect is impossible" to a system that can forget its founder

The System of Eternity (SOE) is a civilizational governance architecture published as Architecture Ready 1.0.0 (S04 Meta Governance, S02 Governance Architecture, S03 Operation Layer). Those documents say what SOE is. This paper records why it looks the way it does: where each main idea came from, what earlier versions tried, what was dropped, and what is still unfinished. SOE began in 2018–2019 as a high-school challenge to the common claim that a perfect world is impossible. Early drafts (2022–2023) promised a utopia run by one moral super-AI. The current architecture is close to the opposite: no permanent center, no ruling AI, no in-system founder role, a small protected core, a protected right to leave, and claims kept inside their evidence. In the author's account, most of that change came from AI models reviewing, simulating and redrafting the design, with one human deciding what was adopted. The paper tags each part of SOE by origin (originated by the author, drafted by AI and adopted by the author, or found by AI simulation), quotes the published R12 text for what each part does, and lists what each part does not solve. It was produced in three steps: a reconstruction by Claude, checks by several AI seats against the published text, and an interview with the author, whose account governs. It was then reviewed by Codex, Grok and Muse Wonder and fix-checked by Codex and Grok. The paper does not argue that SOE works in the real world. The architecture asserts no empirical validation, and neither does this account.
