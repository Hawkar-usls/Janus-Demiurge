Conioscypha liupanshuiensis L. Shao & J. Ma 2026, sp. nov.

Conioscypha liupanshuiensis L. Shao & J. Ma sp. nov. Fig. 2 Etymology. liupanshuiensis , referring to Liupanshui City, Guizhou Province, China, where the type specimen was collected. Holotype. GZAAS 23-0534 Description. Saprobic on unidentified decaying wood. Sexual morph: Undetermined. Asexual morph : Dematiaceous Hyphomycetous. Colonies on natural substratum, effuse, scattered or gregarious, black, glistening, superficial. Mycelium semi-immersed, composed of hyaline to subhyaline, branched, smooth hyphae. Conidiophores micronematous, mononematous, arising directly from the hyphae, hyaline, reduced to conidiogenous cells. Conidiogenous cells phialidic, integrated, sessile or on short conidiophores, hyaline, cylindrical, smooth-walled, with cup-shaped multi-collarette. Conidia 14.5–20 × 10.5–17 µm (x ̄ = 17 × 14 µm, n = 30), acrogenous, solitary, globose to subglobose, obovoid to obpyriform, sometimes irregular, brown to dark brown, smooth-walled, aseptate, guttulate with 1 or 2 - big guttulate, broadly rounded at apex, subtruncate at base. Culture characteristics. Conidia germinated on PDA within 24 h, producing germ tubes from the basal cell. Colonies on PDA were irregular, dense, with umbonate to shallowly lobate margins. The surface was white to creamy white, cottony, with abundant aerial mycelium, slightly elevated at the center, and distinctly concentrically zonate; no diffusible pigment was produced. The reverse was pale cream to light yellow, with a yellowish to buff center and distinct concentric zonation; smooth, with crenate to slightly lobate margins. Material examined. Saprobic on unidentified decaying branches, Liupanshui City, Guizhou Province, China, 29 th March 2023, Lei Shao & Jian Ma, LS 180.1 (GZAAS 23-0534), ex-type living culture GZCC 23-0529. Note. Morphologically, our collection conforms to the generic characteristics of Conioscypha . Phylogenetic analyses revealed that our collection (GZCC 23-0529; Fig. 1) forms a distinct lineage closely related to Conioscypha breviconiophora (KUNCC 24-18128 and KUNCC 24-17971) and C. tenebrosa (MFLU 19-0687 and MFLU 19-0688). Our collection differs from C. breviconiophora in having smaller conidia (14.5–20 × 10.5–17 μm vs. up to 26 × 14–26 μm) that are obovoid to obpyriform, sometimes irregular, and contain one or two large guttulate, whereas C. breviconiophora possesses globose to subglobose conidia. The new collection can be distinguished from C. tenebrosa by its smaller conidia (14.5–20 × 10.5–17 μm vs. up to 25 × 14–20 μm), which are obovoid to obpyriform, irregular, guttulate, and brown (ref). In contrast, C. tenebrosa has dark brown to black conidia and produces conidia through percurrent regeneration of the conidiogenous cell apex at the same level. A comparison of DNA sequence data between our collection and Conioscypha breviconiophora (type strain) revealed 2.2 % variation in ITS (10 / 459 bp, excluding gaps) and 0.3 % variation in LSU (2 / 797 bp, excluding gaps). Comparisons between our collection and C. tenebrosa (type strain MFLU 19–0688) showed 2.6 % variation in ITS (14 / 543 bp, excluding gaps), 0.2 % variation in LSU (2 / 803 bp, excluding gaps), 0.3 % variation in SSU (2 / 797 bp, excluding gaps), and 3.6 % variation in RPB 2 (38 / 1,043 bp, excluding gaps). Based on morphological distinctions and multigene phylogenetic evidence, our collection is considered to represent an undescribed species, which is herein introduced as C. liupanshuiensis following the recommendations of Chethana et al. (2021).
