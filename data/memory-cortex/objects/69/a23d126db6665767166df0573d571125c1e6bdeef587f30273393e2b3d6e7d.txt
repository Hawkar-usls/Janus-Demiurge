The Yang-Mills Mass Gap Options, and the Physical Limits of a Continuous 4D Solution

A positive mass gap remains a viable mathematical possibility in continuous four-dimensional pure Yang-Mills theory, but gaplessness has not been excluded either. We distinguish two proposed counterexample limits: contraction of a gluon organization to zero spatial radius, and softening of an almost-collinear organization through its internal angular motion. Either would exclude a gap if realized as normalized physical states whose complete excitation energy approaches zero in one fixed theory. Their free kinematics do not yet establish that realization. Our physical conclusion concerns a different question. If the multi-fold reconstruction is correct, fundamentally smooth, commutative 4D spacetime is not the microscopic physical case. A fixed minimum spatial radius removes the first limiting sequence; excluding the second requires an additional dynamical bound. We develop complementary arguments, based on quantized flux, confinement, and positive physical spectral measures, and examine how matter changes the question. Massive-quark QCD, chiral Goldstone modes, infrared conformal phases, and radiation have different spectral consequences. Fixed minimum size combined with a positive dynamical cost supports the expectation of a confined gauge-sector gap in a multi-fold universe, not a gap for every matter or radiation channel. The continuous non-multi-fold case remains open for the proposed pure-gauge branches, and can be gapless in appropriate theories with matter. We also discuss the proposed microscopic multi-fold description, its effective Standard Model with gravity, and conventional continuum Yang-Mills theory. Their spectral questions need not have the same answer. We conclude by arguing that, when multi-fold spacetime is discrete, there is a mass gap for Yang Mills carriers, resulting from the minimum distance.
