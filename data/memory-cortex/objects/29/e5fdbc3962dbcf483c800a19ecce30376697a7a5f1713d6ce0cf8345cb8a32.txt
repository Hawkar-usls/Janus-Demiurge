Reproducibility Package for Evaluating Conclusion Stability in SDG Classification: A Provenance-Aware Multi-Model Benchmark of Higher-Education Web Data

Overview This reproducibility package (Version 2.2) accompanies the manuscript entitled “Evaluating Conclusion Stability in SDG Classification: A Provenance-Aware Multi-Model Benchmark of Higher-Education Web Data.” The study extends the evaluation layer of a broader Enumeration-First Information Systems research framework developed for the acquisition and mapping of institutional digital artifacts to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Within this research context, Institutional Host Space (IHS) provides an operational abstraction of institutional web sources, while the present study focuses on the downstream evaluation of SDG classification outcomes. Research Objective The objective is to examine the sensitivity and stability of classifier conclusions under different benchmark constructions, reference-evidence policies, and inferential resampling units. The proposed Evaluation–Conclusion Stability Framework (ECSF) serves as an operational reporting and diagnostic framework that distinguishes five non-interchangeable components of scientific conclusions: model ranking, paired effect magnitude, interval-based inferential decisions, uncertainty precision, and reference-evidence sufficiency. Empirical Scope The study draws on a frozen archive of 20,098 digital artifacts collected from 51 Indonesian higher-education institutions. Four recovered classifier configurations are evaluated: M0: Rule-based SDG classification. M1: TF-IDF with Linear Support Vector Classification. M2: Multilingual sentence embeddings. M3: Multilingual DistilBERT. The evaluation uses two operational benchmarks (A0: 328 artifacts; A1: 400 artifacts), two reference-evidence policies (B0 and B1), and two resampling units (artifact-level and institution-cluster bootstrap). B0 retains insufficient evidence (NSE) as an explicit human-reference outcome and evaluates fixed-18 Macro-F1, whereas B1 excludes human-NSE records and evaluates fixed-17 Macro-F1. These policies define distinct evaluation estimands. Main Findings The descriptive model ranking remained unchanged across the four benchmark-policy states. However, four of twelve cross-benchmark pairwise contrast-shift confidence intervals excluded zero under the recorded exploratory procedure. Across 24 fixed-prediction comparisons, eleven institution-cluster bootstrap intervals were wider and thirteen were narrower than their artifact-level counterparts. Twenty-three comparisons produced concordant zero-exclusion decisions, while one comparison produced a discordant decision at an unchanged point estimate. The H400 reference ledger recorded agreement between two label columns for 393 of 400 artifacts, with adjudication documented for the remaining seven cases (Cohen's kappa = 0.977). These findings demonstrate that observed ranking stability does not necessarily imply stability across all components of classifier evaluation conclusions. Package Contents The package supports the reproducibility and auditability of the reported derived analyses through: Derived evaluation datasets and result tables. Frozen reference and prediction outputs. Analysis scripts and archived bootstrap result ledgers. De-identified reference-label traceability records. Benchmark and evidence-policy documentation. Scoped duplicate-sensitivity and lexical-retrieval audit results. Execution protocols and SHA-256 checksum manifests. README, data dictionary, citation metadata, and version history. Package integrity and reproducibility verification documentation. Version 2.2 aligns the package documentation and citation metadata with the accompanying manuscript while preserving the frozen empirical data, model outputs, analysis scripts, and reported experimental results. Reproducibility and Limitations The package supports derived evaluation and audit reproducibility, including the recalculation of selected evaluation metrics, verification of archived result ledgers, reference-label reconciliation, and integrity checking of the deposited materials. It does not establish independent regeneration of all historical bootstrap outputs or full end-to-end reproduction of historical web acquisition, annotation, and model training. The empirical evaluation is restricted to benchmark construction, reference-evidence policy, and resampling unit. Representation-state variation and cross-domain generalization remain conceptual extensions requiring further validation. The reported confidence intervals are exploratory and have not been calibrated for nominal coverage or adjusted for multiple comparisons. Confidence intervals that include zero do not establish equivalence in the absence of a justified equivalence margin. Reference-label reconciliation and duplicate audits provide scoped provenance evidence rather than certification of independently verified blind consensus, complete training-data isolation, or globally complete semantic deduplication. The package is designed to exclude original web-page content, source URLs, personally identifiable information, and third-party materials with uncertain redistribution rights. Research Context and Intended Use This package supports doctoral research in Information Systems at Universitas Diponegoro, Indonesia, particularly the validation and evaluation of SDG mapping within an Enumeration-First digital artifact acquisition framework. The materials are intended for academic research, methodological benchmarking, reproducibility assessment, and further investigation of evaluation validity in intelligent information systems. ECSF is proposed as a complementary evaluation and diagnostic framework rather than a replacement for the Enumeration-First acquisition framework. The reported findings should not be interpreted as official institutional SDG achievement measurements, IKU 7 scores, evidence of universal classifier superiority, or comprehensive external validation of ECSF.
