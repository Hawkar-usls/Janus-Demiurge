The Illusion of Time - Memory, Consciousness, and the Interpretation of Reality

The Illusion of Time: Memory, Consciousness, and the Interpretation of Reality This independent theoretical thesis investigates whether time is a fundamental property of reality or whether its apparent passage arises from the relationship between consciousness, memory, and the intrinsic ordering of existence. The author's central hypothesis is that experience is always now, and that accessible memories provide the apparent evidence that an individual has moved through time. Reality may possess an intrinsic relational order without requiring an objectively advancing present. Consciousness does not create this order but interprets the relationships between presently accessible records. Originating intellectually from the author's Unified Vibrational Manifold (UVM) research, this thesis examines the separate physical conjecture that time may correspond to a specific wave-function excitation within a globally balanced state. The cognitive hypothesis is formulated so that it can be investigated independently of UVM. The research incorporates neuroscience, reconstructive memory, dream experiences, neurological disorders, temporal perception, quantum mechanics, relational time, and the block-universe interpretation of reality. Mathematical formulations include a finite memory-record chain, a stationary quantum relational-clock model, and an observational-equivalence argument. Seven proposed experimental investigations provide a framework for evaluating the hypothesis against competing explanations through explicit falsification criteria. The thesis distinguishes established empirical observations, conditional mathematical results, theoretical hypotheses, and proposed experiments. It does not claim that the nonexistence of physical time has been demonstrated. Its fundamental objective is to investigate whether reality requires time to pass, or whether memory makes an intrinsically ordered reality appear to pass.
