PATHOGENETIC SIGNIFICANCE OF DISORDERS OF CHOLESTEROL BIOSYNTHESIS, TRANSPORT AND CATABOLISM IN CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASES

Cardiovascular diseases, especially atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction, and stroke, remain strongly linked to disturbances of cholesterol metabolism. This article analyzed contemporary literature on cholesterol biosynthesis, lipoprotein transport, oxidation, and catabolism. The study used a comparative review of international and local publications, clinical guidelines, and open epidemiological data published from 2014 to 2024. The analysis showed that increased HMG-CoA reductase activity, elevated low-density lipoproteins, formation of oxidized LDL, and foam cell development are central mechanisms of atherogenesis. High-density lipoproteins were identified as the main protective system through reverse cholesterol transport. These findings are practically relevant for clinicians, cardiologists, and primary care physicians involved in dyslipidemia screening and rational use of statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 inhibitors.
