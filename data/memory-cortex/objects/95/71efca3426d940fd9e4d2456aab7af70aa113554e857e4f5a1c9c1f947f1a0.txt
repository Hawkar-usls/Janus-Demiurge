Longitudinal Thermal-Passage Phenotypes of Cobetia marina: Experimental Data, Hidden Markov Models, Mixtures of Experts, and Reproducible Analysis Code

Description This release extends the existing experimental data archive supporting “Modeling Sequential Thermal Adaptation in the Marine Bacterium Cobetia marina: Hidden Markov Models and Mixtures of Experts under Progressive Ocean Warming”. It contains the longitudinal adaptive laboratory evolution data for 93 lineages across 15 ascending thermal conditions (35–42 °C), yielding 1,394 usable lineage–passage profiles. Four phenotypic descriptors are analyzed: maximum log-optical-density slope, integrated optical density, maximum OD600, and normalized biofilm (OD540/OD600). The archive includes source data and provenance, fixed lineage validation folds, 136 archived multivariate HMM and mixture-of-experts parameter sets, numerical search histories, 18 local covariance-regularization refits, balanced-budget comparisons, a passage-specific Gaussian benchmark, held-out emission diagnostics, bootstrap and thermal breakpoint sensitivity analyses, and genotype-linked trajectories of eight selected terminal clones. R and Python code and numerical outputs are provided. A unified base-R audit was executed in R 4.6.1 on Windows 11: all seven stages completed successfully; the maximum absolute discrepancy across the 136 archived-model verification outputs was 3.64 × 10⁻¹². The audit verifies archived estimates and does not constitute a complete independent reoptimization. The original data and published detection classifications are retained separately. The experiment does not identify causal temperature effects independently of passage order, or molecular identities of latent states. Keywords Cobetia marina; adaptive laboratory evolution; marine heatwaves; ocean warming; biofilm; multivariate hidden Markov models; mixture of experts; change-point analysis; reproducibility; csgD1
