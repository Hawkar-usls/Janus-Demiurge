Strength statistics, not piezoelectric coupling, limit the power of piezoelectric harvesters in black-hole tidal fields

Piezoelectric harvesters are ranked by their coupling, or . We show that this ranking reverses when the transducer is loaded by body forces it cannot escape. The limiting case is a solid piezoelectric module on a bound orbit around a Schwarzschild black hole, strained by tidal and centrifugal forces. Its axial load is statically determinate, so stress is fixed by the orbit and not by the electrical load. Solving the harvesting circuit over the relativistic load history gives a power density , with near eccentricity . Below a crossover mass this is independent of the black hole’s mass. A slender rod cannot hold the required attitude: 87 % of its starting states tumble chaotically. A cruciform module with inertia asymmetry librates stably and keeps 94 % of the ideal power. Three-dimensional finite elements confirm the bar stress to 0.1 %. Setting the allowable stress from Weibull statistics at a failure probability of , the module’s effective volume, and ten years of slow crack growth, aluminium nitride delivers 16 W from a 0.52 kg module (30.6 W kg ) around a hole. PZT-5H, BaTiO and LiNbO deliver 0.45–1.0 W kg , and PMN-33PT is negligible, because strength scatter rather than coupling sets their limit. Only LiNbO could approach AlN, and only if its Weibull modulus exceeds 15. The modules equilibrate at 17–321 K, so cold, not heat, is the thermal constraint.
