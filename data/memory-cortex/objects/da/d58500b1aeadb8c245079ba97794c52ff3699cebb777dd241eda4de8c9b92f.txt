ChaosFlow: Experimental Evidence and Replication Dataset for Distributed Pipeline Fault Recovery

Overview ChaosFlow is a configuration-driven research platform for evaluating fault recovery, operational resilience, and data integrity in distributed Big Data pipelines integrating Apache Kafka, Apache Spark Structured Streaming, Apache Hadoop HDFS, and Docker. This research dataset documents controlled fault-injection experiments investigating the distinction between infrastructure recovery, service readiness, application progress, sustained-throughput recovery, and independently verified output correctness. Experimental Design The experimental protocol covers eight scenarios: C0: No Fault C1: Kafka Container Stop C2: Kafka Container Restart C3: Spark Worker Failure C4: HDFS DataNode Failure C5: Network Fault C6: Storage Pressure C7: Checkpoint-Related Fault Each scenario is evaluated under observer-enabled and observer-disabled configurations, with 15 qualifying repetitions per experimental cell. The primary campaign records 240 qualifying observations and 16 additional invalid attempts. The broader research includes an independently executed second-machine evaluation, documented in the corresponding experimental evidence. Dataset and Correctness Verification The experimental workload consists of 1,000,000 input records, yielding 966,002 expected output occurrences after deterministic cleaning and filtering. Data correctness is assessed through independent schema-aware typed-row multiset verification and production validation. The research distinguishes successful qualifying executions from invalid attempts, including fault-exposed executions with incomplete output. Research Objectives The evidence supports investigation of: Differences between infrastructure, service, application, and throughput recovery. Data correctness following infrastructure fault injection. Monitoring and observer overhead. Recovery measurement sensitivity and censored observations. Experimental reproducibility across physical computing environments. Limitations of container-health indicators as evidence of application and data recovery. Research Artifacts The deposited materials provide the available experimental records, correctness-validation evidence, recovery measurements, statistical analyses, and supporting audit documentation included in this release. The dataset is intended to support independent research verification, reproducibility, and further investigation of distributed data-pipeline resilience. Limitations The reported findings are conditional on the specified experimental topology, workload, qualification criteria, and recovery-measurement definitions. Qualifying-run correctness does not imply unconditional correctness across all execution attempts.
