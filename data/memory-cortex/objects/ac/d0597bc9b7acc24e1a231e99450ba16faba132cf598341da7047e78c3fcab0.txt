xrMHW: parallel, out-of-core detection of surface and subsurface marine heatwaves

xrMHW applies the marine heatwave (MHW) definition of Hobday et al. (2016) to every cell of gridded ocean datasets: a single time series, a (time, depth) mooring, a (time, latitude, longitude) sea-surface field, or (time, depth, latitude, longitude) reanalysis and model output. Built on xarray and Dask, it processes datasets larger than available memory chunk by chunk on a personal computer and returns the results on the input grid. Main features Complete Hobday et al. (2016) definition: seasonally varying climatology and 90th-percentile threshold, minimum duration of five days, joining of gaps of up to two days, and the categories of Hobday et al. (2018). All parameters can be changed, and a marine cold-spell mode is included. Climatology and detection as separate functions ( xrMHW_clim and xrMHW_events ), so a threshold computed once can be applied to other fields, with the five baselines described by Smith et al. (2025): fixed, periodically updated, detrended, shifting and adaptation-adjusted. xrMHW_func runs both in one call. Daily fields on the input grid: intensity, severity index, duration, category, and maximum and cumulative intensity, ready for connected-component labelling and tracking or for analysis with other gridded variables. Annual and monthly summaries per cell ( xrMHW_annual , xrMHW_monthly ), following marineHeatWaves' blockAverage, and per-cell trends ( xrMHW_trend ) with Theil–Sen or least-squares slopes, Mann–Kendall tests and false-discovery-rate control. Validated against the reference implementation marineHeatWaves (E. C. J. Oliver): the automated tests check that the climatology, threshold, event dates, durations, intensities and categories agree to floating-point precision. Example notebooks with data on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22909109) and reproducible benchmarks on GLORYS12V1 over the Grand Banks of Newfoundland.
