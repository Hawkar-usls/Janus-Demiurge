Machine Learning for Ga2O3 UV Photodetectors: Multi-Tiered Validation Reveals Predictive Limits and Design Rules for Sensing

β-Ga 2 O 3 photodetectors are promising for sensing, but rational design is hindered by their vast experimental parameter space and heterogeneous literature data. We present a machine-learning framework in which all data-dependent preprocessing (imputation, feature ranking, scaling) is fitted inside training folds, evaluated with record-level K-fold, publication-grouped K-fold, leave-one-paper-out (LOPO) and nested cross-validation (CV), and applied to 223 simulation-free devices from 107 publications. Under LOPO, only responsivity (R 2 = 0.26, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.39) and detectivity (R 2 = 0.48, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.68) show demonstrable skill on unseen publications (log10 scale); for EQE, decay time, dark current, on/off ratio and rectification ratio the confidence interval includes zero, and rise time is worse than the training-mean baseline. Record-level K-fold overstates generalization by 0.14-0.65 R 2 units in a matched comparison, mainly because records from the same publication appear in both training and test sets, whereas preprocessing leakage accounts for at most 0.10; in original (non-log) units R 2 is approximately zero for every target. Defect-related descriptors are reported in fewer than 25% of records and cannot be reliably imputed for unseen publications (publication-grouped R 2 = -0.53 for oxygen-vacancy fraction), so conclusions that depend on them are tested on observed values only; the oxygen-vacancy–decay-time relationship does not survive this test. SHAP analysis of models fitted to all records is used descriptively and is compared with observed-only Spearman correlations. Virtual screening of 20,000 candidates ranks designs using only the two targets with demonstrated skill (responsivity and detectivity), flags candidates outside the training domain, and yields design hypotheses, not validated devices; targets without demonstrated skill (for example dark current and on/off ratio) are neither ranked nor reported as predicted values. This work provides a reusable, leakage-controlled framework to extract physical relationships from heterogeneous data, identify critical reporting gaps, and generate design hypotheses for accelerated optoelectronic sensor discovery. Defect-related descriptors are rarely co-reported, so no robust rule can be derived for trap-mediated kinetic metrics (rise time, decay time, rectification ratio) without a minimum reporting standard for oxygen-vacancy fraction, Ga¹⁺ fraction and interface-state density; record-level K-fold values should not be used to judge performance on new publications.
