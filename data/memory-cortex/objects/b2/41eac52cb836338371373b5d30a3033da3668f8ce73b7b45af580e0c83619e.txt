Measuring the Roughness of Quasi-Circular Interfaces at Finite Resolution

Interface roughness characterizes the boundary fluctuations across different length scales, which can reveal information about a material''s pinning landscape and collective interfacial behaviour. Finite sampling and image resolution can bias the measured scaling properties, however. These effects can particularly complicate comparisons between circular domains of different sizes, where the number of resolved boundary points, and the relationship between angular and physical separations vary with the interface radius. Here, we benchmark the recovery of the roughness exponent and amplitude using synthetic self-affine interfaces generated by Fourier synthesis, mapped onto circular contours, and rasterized onto a pixel grid. We first compare displacement-correlation (B(r)) and structure-factor (S(q)) estimates for different interface lengths. To address non-uniform angular and radial spacing in a circular interface, we assess a reconstruction procedure that weights slope minimization against medial interface position. We find that while spectral estimates were most effective in the original interfaces, this advantage did not persist after circular mapping. Moderate smoothing was found to improve parameter recovery, but stronger smoothing can bias both the scaling exponent and roughness amplitude. These results, verified on both synthetic datasets, and an experimental ferroelectric system, identify limitations of circular-interface roughness measurements and provide a basis for further investigations into the evolution of circular domains under finite resolution experiments.
