RECOVAR: Unsupervised Deep Learning for Seismic Event Detection from Continuous Waveform Data

Abstract: Training machine learning models for seismic event detection using regional continuous on-site data remains an under-explored problem. Regional seismic catalogs are often incomplete, and benchmark datasets such as STEAD or INSTANCE have built-in biases in labeling. Unsupervised training on continuous local waveforms, if possible, would have the potential to result in models not limited by such biases, however, established supervised machine learning models such as EQTransformer and PhaseNet are not suitable for this task as they require labeled datasets.We present RECOVAR, an unsupervised learning methodology that can train on extremely imbalanced (99.9% noise) regional, continuous seismic data. The model achieves a detection performance comparable to the state-of-the-art supervised models. The model architecture is based on an ensemble of multiple convolutional autoencoders. Event detection leverages the difference in latent representations between signal and noise samples: after a “dictionary” mapping between the autoencoders, events produce representations with high cross-covariance, whereas noise segments produce uncorrelated latent representations.We develop a dynamic training pipeline that progressively resamples high-scoring segments according to the model's own cross-covariance scores to allow training on noise-dominated, continuous seismic data. Since RECOVAR is not limited by the labeling bias inherent to supervised architectures, it can potentially detect weak or abnormal events that go undetected by other models.RECOVAR achieves a ROC-AUC of 0.97–0.99 on STEAD and INSTANCE benchmarks, comparable to PhaseNet and EQTransformer. We demonstrate RECOVAR's capabilities with two regional applications from Türkiye: on a 3-month-long continuous dataset around the 2019 Silivri earthquake sequence from 17 stations, and on a dataset covering 7 months from the Balikesir-Sindirgi region in 2025-2026, from 6 stations. The Balikesir-Sindirgi region is a particularly active region in Türkiye over the study period. Finally, we demonstrate RECOVAR as a post-processing filter for supervised picks. RECOVAR retains 99% of true picks of PhaseNet while removing half of the false positives. Keywords : deep learning , seismic event detection , Turkey , unsupervised learning
