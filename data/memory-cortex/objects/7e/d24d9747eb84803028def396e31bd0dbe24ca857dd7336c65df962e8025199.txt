Experimental characterisation of compilation-dependent Grover--Rudolph probability distribution loading on a three-qubit NMR processor

Exact state-preparation circuits are normally judged by how closely the measured output distribution reproduces the target. Using the ancilla-free Grover-Rudolph construction compiled into {Ry, X, CNOT}, we ask instead how much the accuracy of a probability-distribution loading depends on which of several logically equivalent compilations is executed. Two Gray-code realisations of the level-2 uniformly controlled rotation, A and B, differ only in the order of their CNOT controls: same unitary, same angles. On a three-qubit liquid-state NMR processor they do not perform identically. Across 700 runs over seven targets the A/B contrast is significant for 3 of them under Holm, with no fixed sign. Two further campaigns isolate the effect within one unit. Removing the circuit's identity-equivalent gates, which changes the executed schedule but not the unitary, affects the two realisations differently: a ladder x intervention interaction of +0.0625 (95% CI [+0.0306, +0.0944], paired p = 4.8e-4), reversing the sign of the A/B advantage. Across five acquisitions of this one logical circuit the contrast runs from +0.0395 to -0.0229. Not every compilation choice matters: commanding the two level-1 inverse rotations as 720 deg - theta rather than -theta, with the gate list unchanged, moves the mean fidelity by -0.0006 ([-0.0157, +0.0146]) on one target in one session. We offer the order and timing of the executed pulses as the hypothesis these observations favour, without identifying a mechanism. The metric does not certify coherences, and the measured correlators are inconsistent with any global depolarising description: on the uniform target, at a classical fidelity of 0.962, a two-body correlation of +0.217 appears where the target has none. Run-level data and code are deposited for every campaign run on our own unit; the pulse-level campaign is reported from summary tables.
