The Zheng–Tse Error Exponents and Optimal Packet Error at Finite Delay

We resolve both parts of Zheng and Tse's 2003 conjecture on coherent Rayleigh fading and their separate prediction for expurgation. We compare independent Gaussian signalling with the optimum over all inputs under the same mean-energy constraint. For the ordinary Gallager function, the gap is less than one nat per transmit–receive antenna pair at every power and positive order. The Gaussian random-coding slope is optimal, and the sphere-packing slope equals outage diversity. A separate comparison proves the expurgated inverse throughout its stated range. At finite delay, we bound the number of messages meeting an error target. The optimum binary error decides exactly when one bit is feasible. For N fades, m transmit antennas and power ρ , consider the error target (1+ ρ / m ) − Nd . The critical value of d is the receive antenna count times the smaller of m and the coherence length. Above this value, the largest feasible payload falls to one message at sufficiently high power. At the threshold one bit is feasible and the rate per use stays bounded. Below it a positive multiplexing gain is achievable. After an explicit logarithmic correction, sphere packing predicts the negative logarithm of the optimal error to within a constant per fade. The comparison is uniform in power, deadline and payloads of at least two messages exactly when the coherence length is at least the transmit antenna count. At 6.02 dB a two-by-two link carrying one bit per fade needs exactly two fades to meet an error target of 10 −5 .
