Determining convex bodies from directly congruent planar shadows

We study the recovery of a convex body from its orthogonal planar shadows when each shadow is known up to an independent planar motion. In every dimension at least three, congruence of corresponding planar shadows by orientation-preserving motions determines a convex body up to translation and point reflection. The three-dimensional argument continues a matching rotation through symmetric constant-width shadows by proving that a neighboring Fourier mode has an isolated zero. This gives a translation or half-turn match in each pencil of planes. Differentiating the matching relation then forces one spatial sign. In dimension three the same uniqueness holds for shadow congruences of either orientation if one odd support part is polynomial. When both odd parts are polynomial, circle means and third Fourier magnitudes alone suffice. For two support functions of degree at most a fixed integer, the discrepancy in these reduced data controls the spatial Hausdorff error modulo translation and point reflection by a degree-dependent Lipschitz estimate.
