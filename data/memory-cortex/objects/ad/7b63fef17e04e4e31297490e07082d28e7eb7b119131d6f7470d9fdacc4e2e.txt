The AI Safety Formalization Atlas: a machine-checked memory for AI safety mathematics

We present the AI Safety Formalization Atlas (AISFA), an open Lean 4 library and ledger that together form a shared epistemic infrastructure for AI safety mathematics: a record of what is proved, from which hypotheses and against which source, with rules for what may enter it. The Atlas has three aims. It formalizes AI safety results that often start as informal arguments. Where it can, it strengthens them, usually by proving them over wider classes of objects or under weaker hypotheses, and occasionally with sharper conclusions. It makes these very different results interoperable, so that they can be composed and a result proved in one domain can be transferred to another. We describe it to invite collaborators. The library holds about 200,000 lines of kernel-checked Lean. AI coding agents wrote the Atlas’s own Lean, and people check the critical points. Each statement that transcribes a published claim is graded against its printed wording: of 316 graded, 201 are stated more generally than print, and eighteen printed claims in twelve sources proved false as printed, each refuted in Lean beside a proved repair. One knowability interface carries impossibility results of one shape across six domains. As a public verifier for nine community solutions to Math for AI Safety open problems, the Atlas checked four, found three partial and two conditional.
