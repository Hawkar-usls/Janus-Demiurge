Magnetic Reconfiguration Controls Spin Splitting and Spin-Orbit-Coupled Responses in Compensated Magnets

Compensated magnets provide a versatile platform for spin-dependent electronic and transport phenomena. Here, we show that magnetic reconfiguration, i.e., selective reversal of local moments without changing the underlying crystal structure, can profoundly transform the nonrelativistic spin splitting and spin-orbit-coupled responses of spin-degenerate antiferromagnets (AFMs), altermagnets (AMs), and compensated ferrimagnets (cFIMs). Strikingly, magnetic configurations with nearly degenerate energies can exhibit qualitatively different spin-split band structures and anomalous Hall responses, allowing a small magnetic-energy scale to produce large changes in physical properties. Combining tight-binding models, oriented spin space group analysis, and first-principles calculations, we demonstrate that converting an AM configuration into cFIM states activates staggered onsite-potential contributions to spin splitting, complementing the anisotropic-hopping mechanism of AMs and removing symmetry-enforced degeneracies. Moreover, cFIM symmetry permits an anomalous Hall response at first order in spin-orbit coupling, in contrast to the higher-order or orientation-constrained responses of corresponding AM states. We demonstrate this regime in Mn2Mo3O8, one of 12 cFIM candidates identified in the MAGNDATA database, and further demonstrate its generality by designing cFIMs from collinear AFMs, exemplified by MnGeO3 and Co2Mo3O8. Our results establish magnetic reconfiguration as a powerful symmetry-guided strategy for dramatically reshaping electronic and transport responses in compensated magnets.
