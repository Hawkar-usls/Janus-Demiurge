HUBBLE CP364 - Teorema 81 - Chiusura Costitutiva dell'Espansione Cosmologica: Formalizzazione Matematica di H_CP364(z)

This work introduces Theorem 81 — Constitutive Closure of Cosmological Expansion , developed within the Trinamica CP364 framework, with the aim of establishing a mathematical connection between elastic time dynamics and cosmological evolution. The research forms part of the CP364 scientific programme and constitutes a mathematical extension of the dossier HUBBLE CP364 — Foundations of Trinamica and Active Time Dynamics (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20666751). Theorem 81 introduces a constitutive structure in which elastic temporal deformation, active energy, temporal tension, coherence, and process memory contribute to defining the dynamic state of the system and its cosmological evolution. The formalization integrates three fundamental levels: Constitutive level: elastic temporal deformation, active energy, tension, and coherence. Dynamic level: Transition Regime (RT), elastic temporal memory, coupling, and process dynamics. Cosmological level: constitutive scale function, Hubble parameter, and connection with cosmological observables. The work distinguishes two levels of mathematical formalization: Theorem 81A — Conditional Kinematic Closure Establishes the conditions under which a positive and differentiable constitutive function of the trinamical state can represent the cosmological scale factor and yield the expansion parameter through its logarithmic derivative with respect to cosmic time. Problem 81B — Dynamic Constitutive Closure Defines the mathematical programme required to determine the cosmological constitutive function through CP364 evolution laws, RT–TEC compatibility, dynamic coupling, initial conditions, and process memory. The formalization includes: The distinction between elastic time, the evolutionary parameter of the Transition Regime, and cosmic time. The mathematical connection between the Transition Regime (RT) and the Trinamical Field Equations (TEC). The relationship between active energy, deformation, and elastic temporal tension. The introduction of an elastic response with a saturation function. The differential formalization of temporal coherence. The integration of elastic temporal memory into constitutive dynamics. The distinction between tensional decay, observational variation, and dynamic coupling. The definition of a candidate family of CP364 cosmological models. The formulation of the expansion parameter H_CP364(t) and its connection with H_CP364(z). The connection with redshift, luminosity distance, and distance modulus. The conditions for existence, regularity, saturation, and transitions between different evolutionary branches. The central contribution of Theorem 81 lies in the construction of a mathematical bridge between the internal dynamics of the Φ domain and the cosmological representation of expansion, while maintaining an explicit distinction between local constitutive quantities, process dynamics, and cosmological observables. From this perspective, cosmological expansion is investigated as a possible manifestation of a structural process in which temporal deformation, energy, coherence, and memory contribute to the evolution of the system. The resulting mathematical structure provides a foundation for the development of quantitative CP364 cosmological models that can be numerically integrated, parameterized, and compared with observational data. The research programme includes the determination of the remaining constitutive laws, parameter identification, analysis of critical regimes and bifurcations, comparison with reference cosmological models using the same datasets, and prospective validation through independent samples. The methodology maintains a clear separation between the different levels of investigation: Observed Data → Mathematical Transformations → Quantitative Results → CP364 Interpretation → Predictive Validation. Theorem 81 represents a further component of the mathematical edifice of Trinamica CP364 and establishes a connection between Trinamica, Chronodynamics, the Transition Regime, Trinamical Field Equations, and observational cosmology.
