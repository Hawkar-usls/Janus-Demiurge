Integrated Channel–Berth Scheduling on a Regulated One-Way Fairway: A Case Study of Nam Dinh Vu Port, Vietnam — Code, Processed Inputs and Computational Results

Zenodo Description — Version 1.1.1 This collection accompanies the study “Integrated Channel-Berth Scheduling on a Regulated One-Way Fairway: A Case Study of Nam Dinh Vu Port, Vietnam.” It provides source code, processed planning inputs, and computational results for a framework that coordinates vessel convoys on the alternating one-way Ha Nam fairway with berth allocation at Nam Dinh Vu Port. The framework combines convoy batching, compatible-berth assignment, and commitment-aware rolling-horizon rescheduling. The study instance covers 1,610 channel movements , including 97 linked inbound calls with berth-service data. The remaining 1,513 movements provide background channel demand. The processed inputs include readiness proxies, service durations, empirical berth compatibility based on vessel length overall, and paired uncertainty realizations. All seven modeled berths are initially available. Outbound channel demand is exogenous to modeled inbound berth completions. Times are measured in elapsed hours from April 1, 2026, at 00:00 in the source planning convention. Version 1.1.1 provides three ZIP archives. The core archive contains research scripts, processed inputs, configurations, saved schedules, and numerical results. The detailed archive provides additional schedules, commitment traces, solver models and logs, and dependencies for selected reruns. The channel-waiting benchmark archive contains a dynamic program for all 1,610 nominal movements, a feasible schedule attaining the channel-only optimum of 483.6 vessel-hours , and comparisons with exhaustive enumeration on 125 small instances. It also provides waiting changes by traffic group, baseline FCFS convoy statistics, and the one-decimal mean values displayed in Figure 2. The README explains how to reproduce the reported statistics from saved schedules and rerun selected experiments from the processed inputs. The channel-waiting benchmark scripts read the core ZIP directly and use Python and a C++17 compiler. The table and figure map links the reported results to their numerical sources.
