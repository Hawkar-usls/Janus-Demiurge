Digital Interventions on Alcohol Use Database

The Digital Interventions on Alcohol Use Database comprises 194 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), including 264 intervention arms, 225 comparator arms, and 298 comparisons. The database was developed to facilitate systematic reviews and meta-analyses examining the effectiveness of digital interventions targeting alcohol use, including comparisons across populations, intervention characteristics, and outcomes. MEDLINE ALL, Embase Classic+Embase, and APA PsycINFO were searched via Ovid on 4 March 2026 and updated on 27 September 2026. The Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) was searched via the Cochrane Library Search Manager on 27 September 2026. Comprehensive data extraction was conducted, including information on authors, publication year, country, sample sizes in the intervention and control groups, mean age, age range, gender, ethnicity, population characteristics, and recruitment setting. We also extracted information on whether participants were in-patients or out-patients receiving alcohol-related treatment, or were recruited from primary or secondary healthcare settings. Alcohol use was assessed based on whether participants were drinking above recommended guidelines and whether this was determined using instruments such as the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT) or recommendations from national guidelines, such as those issued by the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA). We extracted the number of participants with a clinical diagnosis of alcohol use disorder (AUD), including how the diagnosis was assessed. Furthermore, we extracted information on clinical and subclinical symptoms of substance use disorder (SUD), major depressive disorder (MDD), post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety, and personality disorders, including the instruments or methods used to assess these symptoms and disorders. Intervention characteristics included intervention type, intervention assessment and feedback format, number of sessions, intervention duration in weeks, and duration of individual sessions in minutes. We also extracted whether interventions consisted of single or multiple sessions, whether they were of high or low intensity, whether they were guided or unguided, and the delivery mode used. Information on comparator type and whether participants received treatment as usual (TAU), as well as whether participants received additional treatment before or during the intervention, was also extracted. Adherence was assessed based on the percentage of participants who completed up to 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the intervention, as well as the number of sessions or weeks completed. Dropout rates were extracted for the intervention and control groups at each follow-up timepoint. Outcomes were assessed at baseline, post-intervention, and at 1-month, 3-month, 6-month, 12-month, and longer than 12-month follow-ups, where available. The outcomes extracted included total alcohol consumption (TAC; number of drinks consumed within a given timeframe), drinking days (DD; number of days on which any alcohol was consumed), drinks per drinking day (DDD; number of drinks consumed on days when any alcohol was consumed), heavy drinking days (HDD; days on which more than 4/5 drinks were consumed by women/men, respectively), peak quantity (PQ; maximum number of drinks consumed on a single occasion), and abstinence days (AbstD; days on which no alcohol was consumed). For each outcome, we extracted the measurement instrument or scale used to assess alcohol consumption, the number of participants assessed in the intervention and control groups, and the respective means and standard deviations for each group.
