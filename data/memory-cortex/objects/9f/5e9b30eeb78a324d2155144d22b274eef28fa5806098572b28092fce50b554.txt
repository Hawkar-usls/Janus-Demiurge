RENAL FIBROSIS AND INTEGRATED RISK ASSESSMENT IN ADULTS WITH ARTERIAL HYPERTENSION

Background. Renal fibrosis accompanies progression in many forms of chronic kidney disease. In adults with arterial hypertension, measures of fibrosis, routine kidney tests and prediction of kidney failure address different clinical questions. Methods. This narrative review used targeted PubMed searches for studies of hypertensive kidney disease, biopsy-defined fibrosis, urinary Dickkopf-3, diffusion magnetic resonance imaging and kidney failure prediction. Primary studies and nephrology guidance were selected according to relevance to clinical assessment and interpreted with attention to population and outcome. Results. Severe interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy was associated with a 3.42-fold hazard of progression relative to minimal scarring in a mixed native kidney biopsy cohort after adjustment for clinical variables. Elevated urinary Dickkopf-3 was associated with short-term loss of estimated filtration in a mixed chronic kidney disease population. Diffusion magnetic resonance imaging correlated with histologic fibrosis in a cross-sectional biopsy study. These observations support further validation in adults with hypertension. Serial estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine albumin measurements remain the basis of clinical risk assessment. In established moderate-to-advanced chronic kidney disease, an externally validated kidney failure equation provides an appropriate benchmark for determining whether fibrosis measures and inherited risk indicators add prognostic information.
