Genetic parameters of raw flesh colour traits in Amur mirror carp and their relation to performance traits

This study evaluated genetic parameters of raw flesh colour traits in market-size Amur mirror carp and their relationships with key performance traits after the second overwintering and at market size. Flesh colour traits (lightness L*, redness a*, yellowness b* and chroma C*) were measured in 1,277 pedigree-assigned individuals using the CIE Lab system. Heritability estimates were significant and ranged from low to moderate (L* = 0.16, a* = 0.39, b* = 0.23, C* = 0.30), indicating potential for genetic improvement. After the second overwintering, body weight was genetically associated with lower L* (-0.44 ± 0.16) and higher a* (0.43 ± 0.14), while muscle fat content was positively genetically correlated with a* (0.46 ± 0.13), b* (0.48 ± 0.14) and C* (0.58 ± 0.14). At market size, specific growth rate showed strong negative genetic correlations with a* (-0.75 ± 0.08) and C* (-0.64 ± 0.11), but a positive correlation with L* (rg = 0.44). Gonadosomatic index was negatively correlated with L* (rg = -0.65± 0.12; rp = -0.22) and positively with a* (rg = 0.35 ± 0.15, rp = 0.25). These contrasting correlation patterns suggest that estimated genetic relationships between flesh colour and performance may vary with rearing conditions and the relative contribution of natural and supplemental feed as well as changes in fish age, body size, and physiological state. Although flesh colour is heritable, its invasive measurement limits direct application in routine selection. Feeding and pond management may therefore provide more practical approaches for modifying flesh colour, while selective breeding could be considered through family, genomic or indirect selection strategies.
