A sharp stop-loss bound for Rademacher sums, the constant two in a concave comparison, and local extremal sets on the discrete cube

Unrefereed research candidate. Flip some fair coins and give each a real weight, with squared weights adding to one. Add the weights of the heads and subtract the weights of the tails: the result S is a Rademacher sum, centred at zero with variance one. How much of it, on average, sticks out beyond one unit from zero? Kontorovich asked on MathOverflow (question 478477) whether E f(square function) ≤ 2 E f(|sum|) for independent symmetric variables and increasing concave f with f(0) = 0; Lowther (answer 478525) reduced this to Rademacher sums, proved the constant 2.187 and proposed the sharp bound E(|S| − 1) + ≤ (√2 − 1)/2, the value for two equal weights. Results. (1) E(|S| − t) + ≤ (√2 − t)/2 for every t in [1/√2, 201/200], with equality only for two equal weights and a quadratic stability estimate; this proves Lowther's bound, so Kontorovich's comparison holds with the sharp constant 2, also for sign-invariant vectors in any Hilbert space. (2) The constant in Kontorovich's lower bound for the total variation distance between product measures improves from 0.1798 to (1 − e −1/2 )/2 ≈ 0.1967. (3) Fang and Huang's capped three-weight inequality holds without the cap, with stability. (4) On the discrete cube, sets of maximal first-level Fourier weight near density 1/4 are close to two-coordinate faces, and the maximal weight rises by (2 + o(1))h 2 log(1/h) (unconditional). (5) Assuming Lei Yu's 2026 small-density theorem (an unrefereed preprint): an exact formula near 1/4, the next-order terms, and a split of added mass between two neighbouring faces in proportions 0.979:0.021 given by a Curie–Weiss–Potts equation; the analogous results near density 1/8. Limits. The stop-loss bound is proved for thresholds in [1/√2, 1.005] only; the largest possible interval [2 − √2, 2√2 − √3] is a conjecture with numerical evidence. The cube results are local, with neighbourhoods that are not explicit, and the finer ones rely on Yu's unrefereed preprint. Density 1/16 remains open. Contents.
