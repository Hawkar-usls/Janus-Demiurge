FOMO-UDE: Forecasting and Modeling Tumor Dynamics using Universal Differential Equations

Forecasting tumor growth from sparse and heterogeneous clinical measurements is essential for treatment planning but remains a persistent challenge in oncology. Existing mechanistic models rely on fixed biological assumptions, while neural networks typically require large datasets and offer limited interpretability. We investigate adaptive tumor growth models based on Universal Differential Equations (UDEs), which integrate mechanistic structure with data-driven learning to handle limited, irregularly sampled measurements. We conduct a systematic multi-horizon, cross-species study on two publicly available tumor-volume datasets, evaluating the UDE against Gompertz ODE, Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (NODE) baselines under three progressively reduced forecasting regimes. Across cohorts, the UDE consistently achieves the lowest mean RMSE and reduced variance, with reductions of up to 91\% over the strongest baseline. By applying Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) to the learned neural component, we recovered interpretable governing equations, revealing a consistent power-law growth structure that was not encoded into the model. These findings suggest that hybrid mechanistic-neural models offer a principled, interpretable, and data-efficient approach to forecasting structured clinical time-series data.
