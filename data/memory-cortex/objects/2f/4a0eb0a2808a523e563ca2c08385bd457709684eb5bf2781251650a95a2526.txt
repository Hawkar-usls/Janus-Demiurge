Finite hypothesis testing and Neyman–Pearson · 21 additions A randomized test assigns each observation a probability of rejecting the null. For any prescribed size from zero to one, a likelihood-threshold test attains that size and maximizes power among all tests within the size budget. One common randomization probability handles ties, including when the laws have different supports. The minimum sum of type I and type II errors is 1 − TV(P, Q). For equally likely hypotheses the minimum average error is (1 − TV(P, Q))/2. Both minima are attained. Exact-size Neyman–Pearson theorem · A test of size 1/4 and power 3/8 2. Information density and one-shot coding · 20 additions At a specified blocklength and positive message count, the threshold bound gives a supported codebook and a total deterministic decoder. Its uniform-message average error is bounded by the threshold miss under the true input–output law, plus a term for competing messages. The strict mass comparison is defined at every pair. Its logarithmic interpretation requires joint support, and a threshold tie counts as a miss. Under iid input to a memoryless channel, normalized information density converges in probability to the single-use mutual information. One-shot threshold coding bound · Specialize the bound to two messages 3. Operational channel capacity and expurgation · 21 additions For finite channels with nonempty input alphabet, information capacity is the least upper bound of achievable rates, including at zero capacity. One family of codes works for every positive rate tolerance. If uniform-message average error tends to zero, keep every message whose individual error is at most twice the average. At least half the original messages survive, and the maximal error of the retained family tends to zero. At positive blocklength the full retained family loses at most log(2)/n nats per use. Capacity as a least upper bound · Identify the achievable rates of a zero-capacity channel 4. Lossless source–channel separation · 5 additions With one use of a finite memoryless channel per iid source symbol, H(source) < C(channel) gives deterministic encoder and decoder families with vanishing block reconstruction error. The weak converse says that vanishing reconstruction error implies H(source) ≤ C(channel), even when the encoded channel inputs are correlated. Both sides use the same reconstruction experiment. The finite composition bound evaluates the channel error under the message prior induced by the source encoder. The result leaves the equality case open. Direct separation theorem · Meet any positive error tolerance at all sufficiently large lengths 5. Binary prefix coding · 10 additions For a finite source, the expected binary prefix length attains its minimum among codes on the actual support. For three equally likely symbols, rounding ideal lengths up gives a mean of 2 bits. A prefix code with lengths 1, 2, 2 attains the optimum of 5/3 bits. For an iid block of positive length n, the optimal mean per symbol is at least the entropy in bits and strictly less than that entropy plus 1/n. Per-symbol redundancy bound · Compare two-symbol blocks and a ternary code 6. The method of types · 30 additions Raw symbol counts determine a word's type and the multinomial cardinality of its class. At positive blocklength, each word of empirical law q has the same iid probability under a prior p. If the empirical support is contained in the prior support, the class probability is its cardinality times exp(−n[H(q) + D(q ∥ p)]), with matching exponential upper and polynomially corrected lower bounds in terms of KL. An observed zero-probability symbol gives the class zero mass and canonical KL divergence +∞. Entropy and KL here use nats.
