jerlov: inherent optical properties of the Jerlov optical water types, with provenance

Absorption and scattering coefficients for the Jerlov optical water types, taken from the primary literature with the provenance of every value recorded. Values that a published table got wrong are flagged rather than silently repaired, and quantities the data do not determine are refused rather than guessed. Fifty entries are documented, thirteen of them confirmed defects in the source literature, among them: duplicated rows in Solonenko & Mobley (2015) Table 7; a transcription error in the small-particle scattering coefficient that propagated into their published coefficients; a Data Availability Statement that gives the DOI of an unrelated dataset; a reprint whose stated unit is out by a factor of ten; a misprinted equation for trichromatic colour thresholds in Vorobyev & Osorio (1998); a row of Smith & Baker (1981) Table I whose pure water absorption exceeds its own diffuse attenuation; a sign misprinted in Duntley's (1963) equation for the apparent radiance along a slanted line of sight; a second factor of one half printed inside Fresnel's law in Gregg & Carder (1990). Three further entries were open questions that obtaining the 1968 first edition settled. The same Jerlov water type is in circulation with beam attenuations differing by a factor of five, which is why every coefficient here carries the source it came from. Every shipped table can be regenerated from its primary source by the scripts in tools/ , and each generation step checks the result against the equations of the paper it came from. Light through a Jerlov water type. The package: computes the direct and veiling components along a horizontal underwater path, or a slanted one in uniform water (Duntley 1963); converts spectra to CIE XYZ, sRGB, or the response of an arbitrary camera or set of photoreceptors; gives the Jerlov type that typically applies in each 10 m layer down to 200 m, and carries the downwelling irradiance down through those layers; integrates photosynthetically available radiation (PAR), or the photon flux over any other band or weighted by any spectral sensitivity, with depth; gives the spectrum at any depth and the effective Kd between two; and finds the depth at which PAR falls to 1 percent; ranks the Jerlov types by their distance from a measured Kd spectrum; carries the Paulson & Simpson (1977) shortwave penetration parameters used by ocean circulation models; converts a backscattering sensor's single-angle reading into the backscattering coefficient. Measurements it carries. Jerlov's measured profiles of quanta irradiance (350-700 nm) by water type, against which the depth profile is checked; Tyler's (1958) measured radiance distribution in Lake Pend Oreille; the wave-induced light fluctuations measured by Darecki et al. (2011) and Gernez & Antoine (2009), read from their figures; Petzold's (1972) volume scattering functions for eight measured ocean waters; five published spectra of the absorption of pure water, Pope & Fry (1997), Smith & Baker (1981), Mason, Cone & Fry (2016), Lee et al. (2015) and Morel et al. (2007), for the caller to choose from; the eight stations of the clearest ocean water measured, Morel et al. (2007); the measured reflectance of ten materials found in and around water, from the USGS Spectral Library Version 7, for reference images of how they look through each water type. Without a water type. It gives: Kd from the chlorophyll concentration of case 1 water (Morel & Maritorena 2001, and the earlier edition of Morel 1988), or from the absorption and backscattering coefficients and the sun (Lee et al. 2005, 2013); the attenuation of the visible and of the whole shortwave from the surface to a depth, and the depth where a given fraction of the visible light is left, the euphotic depth among them, from the absorption and backscattering coefficients at 490 nm and the sun (Lee et al. 2005, 2007); from chlorophyll, the absorption by phytoplankton (Bricaud et al. 1995) and the backscattering by particles (Morel & Maritorena 2001) of case 1 water; the downwelling distribution function D0 from the sun and the share of direct sunlight, and a Kd rescaled to another light field by Kd/D0 (Gordon 1989); the direct and diffuse irradiance just above and just below the sea surface under a cloudless sky, at every nm from 350 to 700, by the model of Gregg & Carder (1990), with its table of the sun's spectrum and the gases' absorption transcribed and every input describing the atmosphere left to the caller. Ocean colour and measurements. It gives: the remote sensing reflectance from a and bb (Gordon et al. 1988; Lee et al. 2002), and with molecular and particle backscattering kept apart, for the seven viewing geometries of Lee et al. (2011); the remote sensing reflectance of shallow water with a Lambertian bottom in view, straight down, with the bottom's part apart (Lee et al. 1998, 1999); the f and Q factors for a radiance from the nadir of Morel et al. (2002), and their full tables for other views read from the user's own copy of the authors' archive, which it does not ship; the reflectance from radiances measured above the water, with the sea surface's reflectance factor taken from either of Mobley's tables (1999, 2015); the Secchi depth from Kd and that reflectance (Lee et al. 2015); the inversion of the reflectance to the absorption and backscattering coefficients by the quasi-analytical algorithm, in the version of IOCCG Report 5 (2006), checked against that report's published statistics, and as QAA_v6, checked against its author's spreadsheet; the removal of Raman scattering from a measured reflectance by the empirical correction of Lee et al. (2013); the absorption a reflective-tube absorption meter reads, corrected for the light its tube misses, by the four methods Röttgers et al. (2013) compared. Vision. It gives the absorbance template of a visual pigment of Govardovskii et al. (2000) for a peak wavelength and chromophore the caller states, and the distance between two colours in units of receptor noise by the model of Vorobyev & Osorio (1998), for a noise the caller states. Rendering and radiative transfer codes. It: fits one extinction, absorption and scattering coefficient per channel of linear sRGB, ACEScg or Rec. 2020 to the colour of light that has crossed the water, over distances and for an illuminant the caller states, with the error of that fit; gives that transmittance per channel itself, unfitted, at the distances a renderer's table needs; matches a Henyey-Greenstein phase function to one of Petzold's stations by its backscatter ratio or its mean cosine; gives the absorption and scattering spectra at the source's own wavelengths for spectral renderers; gives, from one of Petzold's stations, the share of the scattering within a half-angle and the attenuation of the light that stays within it, for light that a fog attenuating with the beam attenuation counts as lost though it arrives only slightly turned; writes a water's absorption and scattering, less the pure water the code adds back, and one of Petzold's phase functions, as input files for the radiative transfer codes OSOAA and HydroLight, and reads OSOAA's fluxes and radiances back. Findings. Fifty documented entries include: one that computes Kd from measured IOPs for six Jerlov types by Monte Carlo and finds where they and Jerlov's own Kd disagree by more than the unrecorded sun, phase function and pure water can explain, checks it with the radiative transfer code OSOAA, and records that an earlier version made with OSOAA alone was wrong, because the package wrote OSOAA's phase function 4 pi too small; one that checks the same Monte Carlo against the Kd HydroLight computed from Solonenko & Mobley's IOPs; one that traces the remaining disagreement at the blue end to the Kd measured in the same waters. OPEN_QUESTIONS.md gathers such findings, each with the code that recomputes it.
