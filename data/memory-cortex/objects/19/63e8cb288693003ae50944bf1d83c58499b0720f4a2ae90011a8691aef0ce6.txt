Conditioned States as Toric K¨ahler Data A companion note to "Probability, Context and Differentiation: The Layers"

Abstract What is new. The individual tools here are known: Fr´echet bands, Vorob’ev gluing, toric selection, Picard–Lefschetz moves. What is new is one geometric landscape in which a decision maker’s information state is a point that moves as evidence arrives, and every change it un- dergoes is located, typed and given a consequence. The credal fibre shows what the data leave open; the walls of the mirror potential show where the description changes; each wall is classified as forced or computed. A collision of critical values is forced: the two hypotheses are orthogonal and the crossing is canonical, in every fibre dimension. A caustic forces its intersection number, but the side from which it was approached is a convention the data cannot fix. A Stokes event carries intersection numbers that must be computed. Rings of contexts show where gluing fails and by how much. The history of the state records what can be committed at once and what must wait. The landscape is mapped completely for 2 × 3 tables, at the level of critical values for 3 × 4, and by calibration for 3 × 3. It explains how an information state flows; whether it also anticipates decisions earlier than simpler records is an open, registered question. The geometry in brief. A conditioned state is a geometric object. The laws compatible with what has been observed form a polytope, the credal fibre. Its faces are cut out by the cells of the tables, and its vertices are certified by bases that come in dual pairs: the positive cells of a vertex form a spanning tree of the bipartite graph of the table (a graphic-matroid basis), and the zero cells form the complementary cographic basis, whose normals span the vertex cone. Conditioning maps the fibre onto a polytope of conditional laws, and the probability bands we report are coordinate projections of that polytope. Evidence acts on it by a projective map, an isometry of the Hilbert metric. Sampling noise is described differently: under regular sampling conditions it is asymptotically a Fisher–Rao confidence region, whose square-root image on the round sphere is approximately a spherical cap of radius of order m−1/2. Toric K¨ahler geometry supplies the selected law inside the fibre, its scales and its walls; the chamber complex of the margins supplies the stratification along which all of this changes. The note develops this picture and tests every computable claim against exact, pre-registered checks. A running example. A water authority must decide whether to keep seeding storms with silver iodide. It continues if q = P (above-normal rain | seeded, wet forecast) is at least a cost ratio θ. The records arrive in pieces: monthly flight counts, monthly rain categories, and later the 2 × 3 table of seeded and unseeded storms against above-normal, normal and below-normal rain. We follow this one synthetic data stream through the whole construction and back to the decision (Studies 83–90, held-out seeds, every check pre-registered and frozen with SHA-256). The data give a credal fibre and its probability bands; the margins give toric chambers and walls; the margins also set the mirror potential of the degree-six del Pezzo surface, whose thimbles form an exceptional collection in its Fukaya–Seidel category. The experiment is a carrier for a realistic decision problem, not a claim about seeding. 1 What the example verifies. Each layer is tested on held-out data: (i) credal walls lie on the chamber walls, while Stokes events are mutations; (ii) margin-order ties are collision walls where two orthogonal objects swap, confirmed at the Floer level on 10 of 12 brackets (the other two, one not audited and one with a non-minimal representative, stay in the record); (iii) a full twist of the potential acts as the Serre functor; (iv) the support region of a vector-valued hypothesis is cut out by Markov moves on the table; (v) poisoning that preserves the margins cannot move a credal decision, though it flips conventional ones (6 flips against 0); (vi) caustics, where two critical points merge, are A2 folds acting by σ±3; (vii) with two seeding agents (3 × 4, 96 critical points, fibres of complex dimension 5) the same local braids appear on held-out paths, σ3 at caustics and pure σ2 at collisions (30 of 30 loops), and the decision band is still the Fr´echet band; (viii) these local braids, the orthogonality of every collision pair and the sign of every loop are then proved once, by the argument principle and a Morse-chart argument; the hypotheses are certified at 5168 of the 5170 recorded 3 × 4 collisions. What it does not verify. Nothing here concerns real data or whether seeding works. Three problems remain open after a registered repair, with their checks recorded as failed: chamber reuse after one Stokes event, the control bracket of Study 83 (and the held-out bracket with the same defect), and coherence of the lift on a full slice. For 3 × 4 only the strands of critical values are tested, not the thimbles, and three of its six checks failed (two ties where the event detector did not look, and a conditioning floor at L = 30). Seeing the landscape is not yet predicting with it: in pre-registered studies, braid and knot summaries of decision histories added nothing beyond the plain record. Every failed check is kept in the record. Categorical status. In Seidel’s framework the thimbles generate and the identification of their category with the derived category of the surface is proved (lemma 264, corollary 265). Only the wrapped version in the sense of Ganatra–Pardon–Shende still rests on two steps that are argued, not certified. The rest of the note. Part I identifies the state selected by a toric potential as the lexico- graphic max–min point, up to O(1/ log(1/T )), and writes the K¨ahler dictionary in probabilistic language. Part II reads it on contingency tables, where one count of data can cross a wall. Part III treats composition, contextuality, the value of information and poisoned or coordinated sources: trust is relative to a decision, only independent anchors protect it, and braid and knot summaries of a decision history, tested in pre-registered studies, carry the order of exchanges but add no predictive power. Contextuality is the failure of a lift around a ring of contexts, and its minimal defect is the contextual fraction. Part IV classifies the 148 K¨ahler chambers of 2 × 4 tables. The final parts treat stratified credal objects and the mirror of the 2 × 3 and 3 × 3 tables, where Gamma-class central charges are matched to 7 × 10−13 at held-out points, the 3 × 4 table of two seeding agents, and a reading of thimbles as the decision maker’s hypotheses. A full summary of all results, previously in this abstract, is in section 3.1.
