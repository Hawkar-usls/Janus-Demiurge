Open tools for pluvial flood hazard analysis in small rural catchments: A review of rain-on-grid models, terrain-based screening methods and reproducible workflows

Pluvial flooding in small rural catchments is increasingly analysed with rain-on-grid models, in which rainfall is applied directly to a two-dimensional terrain grid. This software review argues that the availability of solvers is no longer the bottleneck. Three other problems dominate: the hydrological closure of rain-on-grid models, meaning infiltration, the resistance to very shallow flow and storage below the grid scale; the conditioning of terrain data with respect to culverts, ditches, roads and field boundaries; and the scarcity of validation data. The review is based on the published literature and on official software documentation; it contains no new simulations. It orders methods into a spectrum from terrain-based screening through simplified two-dimensional models to full shallow-water solvers, and it compares open tools in feature tables in which every entry is taken from a reference publication or official documentation, with unconfirmed properties marked as not documented. It proposes a closure audit, a checklist of what rain-on-grid studies should report, and a reference workflow with the artefacts to be published at each stage. The comparison shows that licences are well documented, whereas rainfall and infiltration options, and parallel execution, are not documented for about half of the solvers. The review closes with research needs, among them closure benchmarks, rules for terrain conditioning, evaluation data for small catchments and documentation of the hydrological options of the tools.
