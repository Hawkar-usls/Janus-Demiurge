Computational Code and Reproducibility Materials for Competence and Confidence in AI-Mediated Higher Education: Longitudinal Evidence of Cognitive Development and AI-Mediated Confidence Displacement

This computational reproducibility archive accompanies the research manuscript entitled "Competence and Confidence in AI-Mediated Higher Education: Longitudinal Evidence of Cognitive Development and AI-Mediated Confidence Displacement." The investigation develops the AI-Mediated Confidence Displacement (AICD) framework through an exploratory longitudinal secondary analysis of cognitive offloading, independent cognitive competence, metacognitive confidence, and their calibration in higher education. The investigation utilizes the publicly available six-wave longitudinal dataset deposited by Shi (2026) in Harvard Dataverse (DOI: 10.7910/DVN/QKL3Q6). The original cohort comprises 1,166 undergraduate students, from which a common balanced analytical panel of 450 students was constructed. Alternative analytical samples and a baseline-eligible weighting population of 1,120 students were employed for sensitivity analyses. The archive contains two executed Python analytical programs, consolidated computational verification reports, machine-readable statistical outputs, developmental and directional bootstrap estimates, software environment specifications, execution instructions, and numerical verification records. The analytical procedures encompass longitudinal developmental comparisons, two-way fixed-effects regression, formal confidence–competence coefficient contrasts, directional calibration decomposition, temporal differentiation, alternative-sample sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, and student-level bootstrap inference. Generative artificial intelligence (ChatGPT, OpenAI) assisted in generating and refining the Python programs under the author's intellectual and methodological direction. The programs were executed by the author using the original longitudinal dataset within Google Colaboratory. Numerical discrepancies and outstanding computational verification limitations are transparently documented. Computational verification is distinguished from independent scientific validation. Harvard Dataverse is designated as the primary computational archive (DOI: 10.7910/DVN/XFIJ09). This corresponding Zenodo archive is intended to support long-term preservation, scientific transparency, accessibility, independent methodological examination, computational reproducibility, and subsequent research extensions. The original longitudinal dataset is referenced through its existing persistent identifier rather than duplicated.
