Zenodo research description This ten-volume research design collection explores whether traditional meridian representations can generate well-posed, falsifiable questions in multiscale biophysics without presupposing a new anatomical structure. The ten flagships distinguish map geometry, cross-tissue graph structure, connective-tissue proximity, nerve geometry, hydraulic networks, interstitial transport, constitutive mechanics, electrical response and incremental statistical evidence. Each retains a separate endpoint and a conventional comparator. Positive predictive utility, if later demonstrated, would not by itself establish a physiological mechanism, independent anatomical channel, therapeutic action, regenerative benefit or quantum advantage. The family is organized as ten independently navigable 777-page draft volumes. Conditional mathematical relations, synthetic numerical grids, five-stage research chapters, future-oriented research gates and candidate reconstruction objects create an evidence architecture. Shared statistical diagnostics deliberately recur as methods rather than being presented as novel laws. The central challenge is to preserve the freedom of subsequent investigators to falsify the candidate or explain an observation through conventional anatomy and physics. The research remains limited to mathematical simulation, digital reference objects and passive or archival designs. Family-level evidence convergence The ten endpoints describe distinct questions: field prediction, curve correspondence, edge prediction, overlap, nerve distance, flow prediction, tracer flux, elastic modulus, impedance and incremental loss. Their native units prevent a common headline score from substituting for the underlying evidence. The family can share a normalized error dictionary only after scale and reference are independently locked. Never use a favorable geometric overlap to compensate for a failed hydraulic or electrical endpoint. Each flagship has independent authority to stop. Future research invitation The most valuable research door may be a decisive negative result: a geometry-preserving null, a conventional mechanism that predicts the same field, or a reference uncertainty that cannot yet be reduced. A future investigator should receive the full failure manifest, source data dictionary and the freedom to build an alternative explanation. This is the meaning of a prospective research contract: not the preservation of a conclusion, but the preservation of the possibility of learning.
