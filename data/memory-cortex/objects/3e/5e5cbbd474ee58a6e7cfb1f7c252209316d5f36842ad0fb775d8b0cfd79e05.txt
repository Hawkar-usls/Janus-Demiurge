TAL

TAL is a high-performance desktop application for crystallographic visualization and analysis, written in .NET 8 with MonoGame. It provides an interactive 3D environment for exploring crystal structures, intermolecular interactions, and advanced computational analyses, with particular strength in the precise detection and characterization of voids and cavities in molecular crystals. Purpose TAL is designed for researchers working with single-crystal X-ray diffraction data. It enables loading, visualization, and quantitative analysis of crystal structures, supporting studies of packing, porosity, host–guest chemistry, hydrogen bonding, and related phenomena. A core focus is accurate mapping of free space (cavities and channels) that accounts for both geometric packing and thermal motion. Key capabilities Structure loading and export : CIF (multi-block), SHELX RES/INS, XYZ, PDB, MOL/SDF, MOL2, Gaussian input, and CRYSTAL17 (.d12). Full write support for most formats, including proper handling of symmetry, ADP tensors, and space-group settings. Visualization : CPK, sticks, balls-and-sticks, ORTEP-style thermal ellipsoids, and coordination polyhedra. Multiple coloring schemes (element, symmetry, molecule, charge, hydrophobicity). Labels, short contacts, hydrogen bonds, unit cell, planes, and symmetry elements (ITA-style axes, planes, and centers). Hydrogen handling : Neutron-standard X–H distance normalization (manual and automatic pathways) and AFIX-aware Uiso recalculation for SHELX files. Cavity and porosity analysis (flagship feature): Signed-distance-function (SDF) voxel grid with flood-fill isolation of buried voids. Anisotropic treatment of atomic displacement parameters (ADPs): cavities are defined against 1σ thermal ellipsoids rather than spheres; symmetry-equivalent atoms carry correctly rotated ADP tensors. VdW radius scaling sweeps with derived QSPR descriptors (fragmentation index, collapse scale, electrostatic/hydrophobic frustration, etc.). Channel pathfinding, sphere-fit packing analysis, gas/solvent compatibility scoring, and detailed single-run or multi-scale reports. Explicit erosion model relating void volume loss to characteristic length (V/S) and thermal amplitude. Additional analysis tools : Hirshfeld surfaces, powder diffraction simulation (Cromer–Mann form factors), mean planes and Miller planes, morphology, structure overlay, H-bond graph-set topology, partial charges (PEOE/SQE), and hydrophobicity. Publication and interoperability : High-resolution screenshots, POV-Ray export of the full scene (atoms, bonds, cavities, planes, polyhedra), and CRYSTAL17 input generation with automatic standard-setting transformation. Implementation notes The cavity engine is deliberately exact rather than approximate: effective radii incorporate VdW multipliers and probe radius; ADP contributions are the physical 1σ half-axes; symmetry operations fully transform both positions and tensors (no diagonal-only shortcuts). Memory and resolution safeguards, smoothing options, and per-cavity visibility controls are included. All major analysis paths produce self-contained textual (and, where relevant, HTML) reports containing parameters, crystal data, and derived metrics.
