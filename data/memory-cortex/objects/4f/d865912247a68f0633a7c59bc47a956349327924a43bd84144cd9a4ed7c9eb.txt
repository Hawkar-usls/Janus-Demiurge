Continuity and Change in Pilgrimage: Visitor Experience, Diversity and Destination Persistence

Despite declining formal religious affiliation across many societies, pilgrimage remains a resilient and publicly visible expression of postsecular religiosity. This article examines that endurance by moving beyond formal measures of belief to explore the practices and visitor experiences through which pilgrimage sustains meaning, engagement and post-visit significance. Drawing on survey data collected during the 2024 and 2025 pilgrimage seasons to Jasna Góra, Poland (N = 667), and a three-month follow-up subsample (n = 130), the study investigates generational differences in a shared sacred journey. Using the categories of affective-spiritual, ritual-community and organisational-pragmatic engagement, it analyses how visitor experiences vary across cohorts and translate into post-visit benefits. Findings identify two related pathways of persistence: continuity, legacy and institutional memory among older cohorts; and organisational quality among younger participants, for whom route clarity, logistical support, safety and structured mobility enable reflection and meaningful experience. The article argues that pilgrimage sites remain relevant through functional plasticity: their capacity to accommodate inherited devotional practices alongside individualised spiritual quests. The findings offer practical guidance for pilgrimage organisers and religious heritage managers seeking to design infrastructure, communication and services for generationally diverse visitors without compromising the religious coherence of sacred sites.
