SPARK Experimental Dataset: Edge-Assisted XR Pose Estimation

This dataset contains the experimental data collected to evaluate SPARK (Task-Oriented Edge-Assisted Architecture for Real-Time 6-DoF Pose Estimation in XR) , an edge-assisted architecture for real-time augmented reality (AR) pose estimation on resource-constrained XR devices. The dataset comprises 40 CSV files containing approximately 28,856 experimental observations , organized into three categories: 1. Pose-estimation experiments (32 CSV files) These files contain runtime telemetry collected under different image resolutions, JPEG compression qualities, marker distances, and motion conditions. The experiments evaluate the impact of visual data compression and downsampling on communication efficiency and 6-DoF pose-estimation performance. Four image resolutions are evaluated: 1920×1080, 1280×720, 640×360, and 360×240. JPEG quality levels of 100%, 75%, 50%, and 25% are included in the compression experiments. The dataset also contains experiments performed at different marker distances and under moving-marker conditions. The recorded metrics include frame rate (FPS), received data rate, pose-estimation time, normalized pose-estimation accuracy, reprojection/pixel error, stability, and tracking accuracy. 2. Client-side performance logs (5 CSV files) These files contain additional runtime measurements collected on the client side during experimental sessions. The recorded metrics include capture time, encoding time, sending time, frame rate (FPS), CPU usage, and memory usage. These measurements provide additional information about the computational and communication overhead associated with the client-side processing pipeline. 3. Network monitoring logs (3 CSV files) These files contain network-level measurements collected during separate experimental sessions. The recorded metrics include round-trip time (RTT)/latency, jitter, packet loss, download bandwidth, and upload bandwidth. They provide additional network-performance observations to complement the pose-estimation and client-side performance data. The recorded network measurements should be interpreted according to their reported metrics; they do not, by themselves, establish one-way application latency or a controlled comparison between transport protocols. Together, these three data categories support the experimental analysis of SPARK, including the trade-offs between visual data compression, communication payload, network conditions, client-side resource consumption, and 6-DoF pose-estimation performance in an edge-assisted XR system. The dataset consists of experimental CSV telemetry only. It does not include raw camera or video recordings. The client-side performance and network monitoring logs were collected in separate sessions, and a direct timestamp-level synchronization with the pose-estimation measurements is not established. Consequently, the additional logs support descriptive performance characterization but should not be interpreted as a fully synchronized or controlled end-to-end evaluation. This dataset is associated with the manuscript: "SPARK: Task-Oriented Edge-Assisted Architecture for Real-Time 6-DoF Pose Estimation in XR".
