Pestalotiopsis alliispathulata Y. C. Xu, Y. C. Tsai & H. A. Ariyawansa 2026, sp. nov.

Pestalotiopsis alliispathulata Y. C. Xu, Y. C. Tsai & H. A. Ariyawansa sp. nov. Etymology. The epithet alliispathulata combines the stem of Allium ( allii- ) and spathulata , referring to the host plant genus associated with the type collection and the spathulate tip of the apical appendages. Typus. Taiwan • Hualien County, Shoufeng Township, on leaf of Chinese garlic ( Allium macrostemon ) ‘ Common variety’ with white spot symptom, 1 May 2022, Yi-Chen Tsai, holotype NTUPPMH 22-143, ex-holotype living culture NTUPPMCC 22-148 (= HLA-29) • ibid ., isotype NTUPPMH 22-228, ex-isotype living culture NTUPPMCC 22-305 (= HLA-29-1). The holotype (NTUPPMH 22-143) and isotype (NTUPPMH 22-228) represent duplicate specimens originating from the same gathering. Description. Based on the ex-holotype NTUPPMCC 22-148 growing on carnation leaves ( Dianthus caryophyllus ) placed on WA. Sexual morph not observed. Asexual morph (Fig. 4): Conidiomata acervular, globose, aggregated or scattered, semi-immersed, black, 250–700 μm diam; oozing globose, black conidial masses. Conidiophores indistinct, frequently reduced to conidiogenous cells; when present, pyriform, hyaline, and smooth-walled. Conidiogenous cells ellipsoidal to fusiform, hyaline, smooth-walled, (7.2 –) 7.9–11 (– 12.5) × (3.0 –) 3.2–4.6 (– 5.3) μm, x ̄ ± SD = 9.4 ± 1.6 × 3.9 ± 0.7 μm. Conidia fusoid, straight or slightly curved, 4 - septate, smooth-walled, slightly constricted at the septa, (32.3 –) 33.9–37.7 (– 40.2) × (5.4 –) 5.9–7.0 (– 8.4) μm, x ̄ ± SD = 35.8 ± 1.9 × 6.4 ± 0.6 μm, bearing appendages; basal cell obconic with a truncate base, hyaline or pale brown, thin-walled, (5.9 –) 6.7–8.8 (– 9.9) μm long, x ̄ ± SD = 7.7 ± 1.1 μm; three median cells elongate doliiform to subcylindrical, pale brown to olive brown, concolourous, thick-walled, the first median cell from base (5.6 –) 6.5–7.8 (– 8.3) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 7.2 ± 0.6 μm), the second median cell (6.0 –) 6.5–7.8 (– 9.1) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 7.1 ± 0.6 μm), the third median cell (6.4 –) 6.9–8.2 (– 9.4) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 7.5 ± 0.7 μm), together (19.8 –) 21.3–24.4 (– 27.2) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 22.8 ± 1.5 μm); apical cell conical with a truncate apex, hyaline, thin-walled, (3.4 –) 4.4–5.5 (– 6.1) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 5.0 ± 0.6 μm). Appendages tubular, hyaline, straight or slightly bent, apical appendages 2–3, not arising from a common apical crest, unbranched, swollen at the tip (spathulate), (14.3 –) 17.6–23.0 (– 27.3) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 20.3 ± 2.7 μm); basal appendage single, centric, unbranched, (4.2 –) 5.1–6.8 (– 7.6) μm long (x ̄ ± SD = 6.0 ± 0.9 μm). Culture characteristics. Colonies on PDA attaining 49–54 mm in diam. after seven days at 25 ° C in darkness, irregular to stellate, raised, with lobate margins; surface whitish, covered with dense aerial mycelia; reverse yellowish at the centre and whitish towards the margins. Notes. Pestalotiopsis alliispathulata , a novel taxon proposed in this study, exhibits the typical morphological characteristics of Pestalotiopsis in having pale brown to olive-brown, concolourous median cells. However, this species is distinctive in producing knobbed (spathulate) apical appendages, a character that is relatively uncommon within the genus. To date, similar appendage morphology has only been reported in several relatively basal taxa of the Pestalotiopsis clade, including P. ericacearum , P. jesteri , P. kunmingensis , P. pallidotheae , P. spathulata , and P. spathuliappendiculata (Suppl. material 1: table SS 2), although these species do not form a monophyletic lineage (Liu et al. 2019; Maharachchikumbura et al. 2014). In both single-locus and concatenated phylogenetic analyses, the two strains of P. alliispathulata formed a distinct lineage (MLB = 100 %; MPB = 100 %; BPP = 1.00; aBayes = 1.00). Species boundaries between P. alliispathulata and its closely related taxa were further evaluated based on the GCPSR principle using both single-locus and multilocus phylogenetic analyses. In the concatenated phylogeny, P. alliispathulata formed a sister lineage to P. arengae (MLB = 93 %). In the ITS phylogeny, P. alliispathulata was recovered as sister to P. aporosae-dioicae (MLB = 99 %). In the tef 1 - α phylogeny, P. alliispathulata clustered in a basal position relative to all Pestalotiopsis taxa except P. aporosae-dioicae (MLB = 87 %). In the tub 2 phylogeny, P. alliispathulata formed a basal lineage to the clade comprising P. arengae and P. aporosae-dioicae (MLB = 100 %; aBayes = 1.00). Collectively, these results demonstrate that P. alliispathulata is genetically distinct (Suppl. material 1: table S 3;> 70 nucleotide differences from each of the closely related species across the three-locus dataset when gaps were included) and consistently supported across all phylogenetic analyses, thereby satisfying the GCPSR criterion of genealogical non-discordance. Furthermore, PHI test analyses detected no significant recombination signal (Φ w = 0.7823) among P. alliispathulata and its closely related taxa (Fig. 5). Together with the substantial sequence divergence and distinct phylogenetic placement, this result is consistent with the recognition of P. alliispathulata as an independently evolving lineage. Based on the combined evidence from morphology, phylogenetic analyses, and GCPSR evaluation, this study introduces Pestalotiopsis alliispathulata (NTUPPMCC 22-148 and NTUPPMCC 22-305) as a novel species within the genus Pestalotiopsis .
