The Receiver Needs Phase and Orientation

This report presents Stage 3 of a computational investigation into Bell correlations and observer-resolved accessibility within a Phase-prior research programme. It examines how a receiver’s polarization response changes the correlations accessible to measurement while the input source state remains fixed. A conditional optical bridge is derived from a supplied two-mode propagation generator to the receiver transformation, measurement effects and joint outcome probabilities. For a lossless retarder followed by a two-port linear analyser, the accessible Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt (CHSH) maximum depends on both relative phase delay and receiver eigenaxis orientation. Identical retardance can therefore produce different accessible Bell correlations without changing source entanglement. The original study covers 42 receiver configurations, three source/control states and 40,824 grid probabilities, supported by an analytic maximum over the full admitted measurement family. Version 1.1 adds an amplitude-weighted extension with 12 principal cases, 24 control cases and 11,664 grid probabilities. The 70/30 and 90/10 preparations retain entanglement and yield aligned-receiver CHSH values of approximately 2.713 and 2.332, respectively. These weights are declared model inputs. All specified numerical checks passed. Separable controls demonstrate that restricted measurements can reproduce the accessible statistics of an entangled source. The distinction between source entanglement and its accessibility therefore remains essential. The study provides a reproducible benchmark within the stated quantum-optical model. The Phase-prior interpretation assigns the joint state to nesting and its accessible records to observation coupling; a Phase-specific law deriving receiver response from nested organisation remains unresolved. Results are deterministic model calculations, with no experimental measurements or fitted data. The accompanying archive includes protocols, code, numerical outputs, controls, execution records, source provenance and checksums. Version 1.1, dated 20 October 2026, preserves the original report and appends the unequal-weight results.
