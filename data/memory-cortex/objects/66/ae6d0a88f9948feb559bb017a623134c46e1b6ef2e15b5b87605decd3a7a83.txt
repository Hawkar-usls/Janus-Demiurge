Satellite Technology Explained: Systems, Communication & Space Applications

This academic curriculum module delivers an analytical, physical, and computational exposition of satellite technology, Keplerian orbital mechanics, communications link budgets, and spacecraft subsystems across Low Earth Orbit (LEO), Medium Earth Orbit (MEO), and Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) environments. Key Technical Topics & Curricular Areas Covered: 1. Orbital Mechanics & Mission Design: Circular velocity derivations (v = sqrt(mu/r)), Kepler's Third Law orbital period determinations, Geostationary altitude sizing, and two-impulse Hohmann transfer delta-v maneuvers. 2. Space Communications & RF Link Budgets: Free-space path loss (FSPL) formulations across C-band, Ku-band, and Ka-band frequencies; antenna gain trade-offs; receiver noise figure thresholds; and rain fade atmospheric attenuation. 3. Satellite Subsystems Architecture: Attitude Determination and Control Systems (ADCS) using reaction wheels and magnetorquers; space power systems utilizing photovoltaic arrays and battery storage under orbital eclipse cycling; and thermal management via radiators and multi-layer insulation (MLI). 4. Modern Computational & AI-Era Paradigms: Machine-learning-augmented orbital perturbation propagators (ML-SGP4) correcting for solar-storm atmospheric drag surges; deep reinforcement learning for autonomous in-orbit collision avoidance; and edge AI hyperspectral compression on space-grade FPGAs. 5. Contemporary Systems Engineering Trade-offs: LEO mega-constellations vs. GEO wide-area platforms, optical inter-satellite links (ISLs) vs. RF ground relays, electric Hall-effect thrusters vs. chemical monopropellants, and standardized CubeSat modularity vs. high-reliability radiation-hardened redundancy. 6. Relativistic Physics in Space Systems: Gravitational time dilation and special relativity corrections essential for sub-meter Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS). 7. Pedagogical Features: An interactive web-based orbit velocity and orbital period calculator with dynamic trajectory visualization, structured conceptual reviews, and step-by-step worked numerical engineering problems with explicit physical units.
