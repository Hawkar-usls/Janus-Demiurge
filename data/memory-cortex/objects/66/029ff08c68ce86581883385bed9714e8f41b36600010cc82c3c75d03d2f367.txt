Feed-rate metrology and data provenance for dosing-controlled sediment experiments: A recommended practice

The sediment supply of a laboratory flume is usually specified by the set point of a doser, although the quantity that the experiment receives is a feed rate, a result of measurement that carries an uncertainty, depends on the time over which it is averaged, and is only as trustworthy as the record of how it was obtained. This recommended practice turns published principles of metrology and research data management into a compact procedure for experiments in which sediment is dosed. It uses the definitions of the International Vocabulary of Metrology and the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement, and derives measurement models for volumetric and gravimetric feeding, the relation between the metered and the delivered rate, an uncertainty budget by the law of propagation of uncertainty, and the relation between the duration of a timed catch and its uncertainty. It transfers the Allan deviation from frequency metrology to feed-rate series as a way of reading an averaging time, proposes checks of the grain-size distribution that is actually delivered, addresses time keeping, and defines a minimum provenance record and a reporting checklist. A further clause orders the recommendations into a practical sequence before a campaign, before and during each run and after it, and applies them to a loss-in-weight vibratory feeder for bed-load sediment, using the functions documented by its manufacturer: tare, friction test, span and feed-factor calibration, accuracy check, and refill in volumetric mode. The practice is set out in numbered clauses, seventeen numbered recommendations and three annexes with a budget template, a metadata record and a checklist. It is a synthesis of published methods, contains no measurements or experimental results, does not claim the status of a standard, and states the limits of its analytical relations.
