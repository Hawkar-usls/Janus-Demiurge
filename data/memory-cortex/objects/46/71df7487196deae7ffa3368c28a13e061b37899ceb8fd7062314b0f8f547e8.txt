Sediment supply in laboratory experiments: A review of feeding and dosing techniques, their control, and their consequences for experimental results

The supply of sediment is a boundary condition of laboratory experiments that is as consequential as the discharge, yet it is specified and reported less rigorously. This review synthesises published work on how sediment is supplied to flumes and overland-flow experiments, how the supply is controlled, and what follows for experimental results. Experiments on feed and recirculating operation, on the rate, grain size and temporal pattern of the supply, and on unsteady flow indicate that the supply governs equilibrium, bed texture and the response to change, and that feeding and recirculation are not equivalent for mixed-size sediment. A typology of supply methods and their characteristic errors is proposed, and knowledge on volumetric and loss-in-weight feeding, hopper refill, segregation and moisture is transferred from bulk-solids and pharmaceutical engineering. The uncertainty of a feed rate estimated from weighings is shown to fall in inverse proportion to the length of the averaging window, so that responsiveness and precision trade off. The documented functions of a current loss-in-weight vibratory feeder for bed-load sediment are read against these findings: its wide range of rates, the volumetric operation during hopper refill, and the values it makes available for recording. A conceptual time-scale argument, extending the inlet boundary layer known from experiments with cycled hydrographs, indicates when fluctuations of the feeder are absorbed by the bed, when they cannot be separated from intrinsic fluctuations of bedload transport, and when the bed follows them. The review closes with a checklist for reporting the supply boundary condition, summary points and open questions. The review accompanies the development of a browser-based interface for that feeder. The synthesis is literature-based; the time-scale argument is a proposal that remains to be tested with feeders.
