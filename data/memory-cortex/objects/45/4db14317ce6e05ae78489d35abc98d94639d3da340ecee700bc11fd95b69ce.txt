One curve at two scales: the tractrix of the cosmic expansion and of the galaxy law

A route to a known result, not found in the literature searched: MOND's 'simple' interpolating function, nu = 1/2 + (1/4 + a0/g_N)^(1/2), whose boost at g_N = a0 is the golden ratio, follows exactly from the same curve that traces the cosmic expansion, given two stated inputs. The function itself is not new (Famaey and Binney 2005), and it has recently been obtained approximately by a different route, from the cosmic and Rindler horizons (Urueña Palomo 2026); the route through the expansion history's own curve was not found in the literature searched. The curve: for a flat universe of matter and a constant push, the square root of the matter fraction traces a tractrix; if Omega_m = 1/pi, its leash stands at 55.65 degrees from the vertical today. McGaugh's galaxy law can be written on the same curve. The two inputs are the boost read off the curve and the postulate that a galaxy is placed on the curve by the same rule as the universe. Both inputs were taken up after the fact, and a0 is imposed, so the note records a conditional derivation, not a prediction. Omega_m = 1/pi is the programme's preregistered value; it was published earlier by another author and chosen when Omega_m of about 0.31 was known (see the programme overview). Other earlier work cited: Pazy and Argaman 2012, and Milgrom 1999 and 2009. Further exact readings, found after the fact, are given: - the involute pair, catenary and tractrix, whose squared heights are pi and 1/pi today; - the rim of the pseudosphere (the circle where its two halves meet) as the Big Bang; this is not the force-balance 'rim' of the deposited rim note r3b; - the observed, Newtonian and memory fields as the growing, full and decaying modes; - the equation g (g - g_N) = a0 g_N, which is equivalent to the law and derives nothing. An open ambiguity, stated after these notes were frozen: where the Big Bang sits on the hyperbolic horn. In this note the curve plots the height sqrt(Omega_m), which is 1 at the Big Bang, so the Big Bang falls on the widest circle (the rim), and the surface narrows toward the de Sitter end state. The companion note 'A dictionary between a 4D-fall cosmology and the mod-210 prime corpus: zeros as resonances of a postulated cusp at the Big Bang rim' (deposited on Zenodo alongside this one) instead postulates the Big Bang at the cusp of the modular surface, where the closed circles shrink to nothing. The author's view, which he says he has always held, is the cusp: the Big Bang starts at the cusp, a point, as general relativity reduces the universe to a point, and the mirror history (the author's origin ruling) joins ours there. One reading, offered by the drafter (Claude) and not by the author, is that the one-curve rim then marks the starting value of the matter fraction rather than a size. None of this is derived or tested, and the notes are not revised for it. Two tests designed before computing found, as expected, no system in the picture that supplies the modes or the placement. A consistency check on SPARC data (already used) finds the simple function indistinguishable from McGaugh's: a0 is 1.11 vs 1.14 e-10 m/s^2, and the scatter is 0.131 dex for both. Solar-system limits from Cassini apply to modified-gravity MOND, not to this model. The author has adopted the simple function as the galaxy law of the model (version r7f, not deposited), with a0 unchanged at 1.2e-10 m/s^2. The note makes no prediction. It was drafted with Claude (Anthropic) and reviewed by independent sessions of the same model.
